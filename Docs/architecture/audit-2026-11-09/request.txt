## 3. Source Authority and Classification

Distinguish:

- `Requirement`: explicitly required by the official brief or dataset contracts.
- `Accepted`: recorded team architecture decision.
- `Proposed`: an unapproved engineering recommendation.
- `Open`: unresolved decision or genuine conflict.
- `Observation`: verified evidence from project data or audit results.

Observation is an evidence category, not an approval status.

Previous Codex outputs, architectural proposals, and audit interpretations are not automatically authoritative.

When sources conflict, preserve the conflict and identify the exact source locations. Do not invent a resolution.

## 7. Documentation Correction Rules

Make evidence-backed corrections to the architecture documentation.

- Preserve correct and useful existing content.
- Correct omissions, contradictions, inaccurate claims, and misleading terminology.
- Avoid unnecessary large-scale rewriting.
- Preserve original source files and accepted decisions.
- Never silently promote Proposed details to Accepted.
- Never mark an acceptance test as passed without execution evidence.
- Do not invent missing business rules, owners, thresholds, or formulas.
- Preserve stable ADR, REQ, and AT identifiers unless a justified migration is necessary.
- Review the repeated `ARCH-001` identifiers and establish whether they are document IDs or a shared architecture-family identifier before changing them.
- Separate project requirements from implementation recommendations.

Do not modify the official brief, original dataset, reference SQL, original KPI dictionary, or source contracts.

No product implementation is authorized in this task.
